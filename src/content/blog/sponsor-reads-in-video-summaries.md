You know the moment. The video is getting good, and then: "But first, a word from today's sponsor." Ninety seconds about a VPN, a meal kit, or a mattress, then a promo code, then back to the actual content.

When you summarize that video, the sponsor read has to go somewhere. We've mentioned before that AI Recaps handles it a certain way, in [how we decide what makes the cut](/blog/how-ai-recaps-decides-what-makes-the-cut). This post is the longer version: where the ad goes, and why we didn't just delete it.

## Out of the Summary

The first rule is simple. Ad reads, sponsor segments, and self-promotion stay out of the summary content. That covers the TL;DR, the key points, the full summary, and the notable quotes.

That matters more than it sounds. If a summary is built from the transcript and the transcript includes 90 seconds of "this video is brought to you by," a careless summary can end up treating that as part of the story. You'd get a key point about how much the host loves their password manager, sitting next to the actual findings. A summary is supposed to tell you what the video says. The ad isn't what the video says.

Self-promotion gets the same treatment. "Check out my course," "join the Patreon," "hit subscribe": none of it ends up in your summary.

## Into Its Own Section

The second rule is the one people don't expect. When a video mentions sponsors, the summary ends with a short section called Sponsors.

It lists each sponsor by name, with a brief description if the creator gave one, and the promo code or link if they mentioned it. If a video has no sponsors, there's no section at all. You won't see an empty header.

So the sponsor isn't erased. It's moved out of the way.

## Why Not Just Delete It?

There are good tools for skipping sponsors. SponsorBlock, for example, is a crowdsourced browser extension: once someone marks a sponsored segment, everyone else using it skips right over it. If you're watching, that's a reasonable thing to want.

A summary is different, for three reasons.

**Sponsors are how a lot of creators get paid.** We summarize other people's work. Keeping a short, clearly separated note of who paid for the video feels like the fair middle ground. You don't sit through the pitch, and the creator's sponsor still gets mentioned.

**Sometimes you want the code.** A creator you trust recommends something, mentions a discount, and you forget the code by the end of the video. With the Sponsors section, it's sitting at the bottom of the summary.

**It tells you something about the video.** YouTube has creators flag paid promotion, and some videos carry an "Includes paid promotion" label. If you're reading a summary of a review, it's useful to know that the company being reviewed, or a competitor, sponsored it. A summary that quietly removed that would hide something you'd want to know.

## What It Can Miss

The summary works from what's said in the video. A sponsor that's only shown on screen, like a logo in the corner or a product sitting on the desk with no mention, won't show up in the Sponsors section. And if a creator works a product into the content naturally without calling it a sponsorship, the summary can't tell the difference either. Check the video's description if you want the full disclosure.

## The Bottom Line

The ad read doesn't belong in the summary, and it doesn't vanish either. It goes to the end, in its own section, where you can skip it or use it.
