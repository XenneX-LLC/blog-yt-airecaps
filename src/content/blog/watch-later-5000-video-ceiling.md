Watch Later starts with good intentions. You save a video because it looks worth your time and you don't have time right now. Then you save another, and another, and a year later the list is hundreds of videos long and you haven't opened it in months.

There's also a hard limit. Watch Later holds up to 5,000 videos, the same cap as any YouTube playlist. Most people never hit it. But the list usually stops working long before that, because a playlist you never get through isn't a plan. It's a pile.

Here's how to clear it without watching everything on it.

## Why Watch Later Only Grows

Saving a video takes one tap. Watching it takes 20 minutes, or two hours. That math means the list grows faster than you can work through it, every single week.

It gets worse with age. The longer a video sits there, the less likely you are to watch it, and the harder it is to remember why you saved it. Old saves bury new ones, so even the videos you'd actually want to watch get lost under the backlog.

## Start With the Easy Win

Before you triage anything, clear out what you've already seen. On the Watch Later page, open the three-dot menu at the top of the playlist and choose "Remove watched videos." It removes everything YouTube has marked as watched in one go.

That's the only bulk option built in. Everything unwatched has to be removed one video at a time, which is exactly why the rest of the list needs a faster way to decide.

## The Triage Method: Summarize, Decide, Delete

Work through the list in batches of 10 to 20, starting with the oldest saves. For each video, do three things.

**1. Summarize.** Don't watch it. Read a summary of it first. In a minute or two, you'll know what the video actually says, which is far more than the title and thumbnail told you when you saved it.

**2. Decide.** Most videos land in one of three buckets:

- **The summary was enough.** You got the key points, and watching the full video wouldn't add much. Done.
- **Worth watching in full.** The summary made you want more: a demo you need to see, a conversation with real depth, something you'll enjoy as video. Keep it.
- **No longer relevant.** The news is old, the product changed, or you're not interested anymore. Done.

**3. Delete.** Remove everything in the first and third buckets. Only the "worth watching in full" videos stay.

Most people find the first and third buckets are most of the list. That's the point: the backlog was never really a list of videos you needed to watch. It was a list of videos you needed to decide about.

## Keep It From Coming Back

Clearing the list once feels great. Keeping it clear takes a couple of habits.

**Summarize before you save.** If you check the summary when you first find a video, you'll often realize you already have what you need, and the video never goes on the list at all.

**Set a small cap.** Pick a number you can actually get through, like 25 or 50. When you hit it, triage the oldest few before adding more.

**Do a weekly pass.** Ten minutes a week with the same summarize, decide, delete routine keeps the list from turning back into a pile.

## The Bottom Line

You don't need to watch your whole Watch Later list to clear it. You need to decide about each video quickly, and a summary is the fastest way to do that. Clear out what you've watched, triage the rest in small batches, and keep only the videos that earn your time.
