A creator you like is in the middle of a series. A build that's taking months, a documentary in parts, a playthrough, a video essay that turned into five videos. You watched the first one or two, fell behind, and now the next part comes out this week.

You have two options that don't work. You can rewatch everything from the start, which is a whole evening for a video you haven't even gotten to yet. Or you can jump into the new part cold and spend the first ten minutes trying to remember who's who and what went wrong last time.

There's a third option. Read the parts you missed, then watch the new one properly.

## Step One: Find the Parts

Most creators who run a series keep it in a playlist, and the playlist is usually linked in the description of each part. Open it and you have the order, the titles and the release dates in one place.

If there's no playlist, search the channel for the series name. Make a quick list of the parts you haven't seen.

## Step Two: Summarize Them in Order

In AI Recaps, open **Summarize Now**. Search for the video, or tap "Can't find your video? Paste a URL instead" and paste the link from the playlist. Hit **Generate**, then do the same for the next part.

Do them in order, oldest first, and read each summary before you move to the next. A series builds on itself. What part 3 tells you makes more sense once you know what part 2 set up.

Each one counts as a summary toward your plan's monthly limit, so a four-part catch-up uses four.

## Step Three: Write Down What's Still Open

As you read, keep a short list of what's unresolved: the problem that hasn't been fixed yet, the question the creator said they'd come back to, the decision that's still up in the air. That list is what you're carrying into the new part.

If you're on Pro, you can ask the summary directly. Open a summary and use the chat to ask things like "what was still broken at the end?" or "what did they say they'd try next?" It answers from that video, so you don't have to scrub through it to find the one moment you need.

## Step Four: Watch the New Part in Full

This is the one you actually sit down for. You know where the story is, so you can just watch it.

A summary gives you what happened. It doesn't give you the pacing, the reveal or the way the creator tells it, and for a series, that's often the point. We wrote about that tradeoff in [what a good summary catches and what it misses](/blog/what-a-good-summary-catches-and-misses). Use summaries to get caught up, not to replace the episode you were waiting for.

If a summary of an earlier part makes you want to see it after all, go watch it. That's a good result, not a failure.

## Step Five: Don't Fall Behind Again

If the series is still going, add the channel to a schedule. New uploads get summarized as they come out, so if life gets busy again you'll have the summary waiting instead of another backlog. Pick a frequency that fits how often the creator posts. Our post on [picking your summary schedule](/blog/picking-your-summary-schedule) walks through the options.
