A summary isn't a shorter copy of a video. It's a series of decisions about what matters most, what supports it, and what can go. We've written before about [what a good summary catches and what it misses](/blog/what-a-good-summary-catches-and-misses). This post is about the other side of that: how AI Recaps actually decides what makes the cut.

## It Starts With More Than the Transcript

The transcript is the main input, but not the only one. The video's title, channel, and description go in too. That context matters more than it sounds like it should. A title tells you what the creator thinks the video is about. The description often has links, names, and details that never get said out loud. Reading all of it together helps the summary lead with what the video is actually trying to deliver.

## The Structure Is the First Filter

Every AI Recaps summary follows the same shape, and each section forces a different kind of choice.

**TL;DR.** Two or three sentences with the core argument or takeaway. This is the hardest cut to make, and the most useful one: if you read nothing else, you should still know what the video was about and whether it's worth more of your time.

**Key Points.** Three to five of the most important claims, findings, or arguments, each written as a specific statement rather than a vague topic. "The new model is 40% faster on long documents" makes the cut. "They talk about performance" doesn't. Numbers, names, and specifics get priority here, because they're the details people actually come back for.

**Summary.** The fuller picture: the whole arc of the video with the context you'd need to understand why it matters. Its length scales with the video, so a ten-minute explainer gets a short read and a three-hour conversation gets a longer one.

**Notable Quotes.** A few direct quotes that capture a key moment in the speaker's own words. The bar is "genuinely notable," not "technically a quote." If nothing clears it, the section is left out entirely instead of being padded with filler.

## What Gets Adjusted by Video Type

Not every video should be summarized the same way, so a few types get handled differently.

**Product reviews lead with the verdict.** Most people watching a review want to know one thing first: is it worth buying? The summary opens there, then backs it up with the specs and comparisons.

**Tutorials follow the steps.** A tutorial's value is the process, so the summary is organized around the steps being taught rather than around a single takeaway.

**It matches the video's language.** A summary is written in the same language as the transcript, so it reads naturally for the person who'd actually watch that video.

## What Gets Cut on Purpose

**Ad reads and self-promotion.** Sponsor segments are left out of the summary content. When a video does mention sponsors, they're listed in their own short section at the end, so the information is there if you want it but never mixed in with the actual content.

**Filler and throat-clearing.** Summaries are written directly, as a briefing, not as a description of a video. You won't see "in this video, the host discusses..." The goal is to tell you what was said, not that something was said.

**Generic statements.** Anything that could apply to almost any video on the topic is the first thing to go. If a point isn't specific to this video, it isn't earning its space.

## The Bottom Line

Good summarization isn't about compressing everything evenly. It's about deciding what matters most and giving it the most space. AI Recaps puts the core takeaway first, backs it with specific points, fills in the full story for anyone who wants it, and deliberately leaves out the parts that were never the point of the video to begin with.
