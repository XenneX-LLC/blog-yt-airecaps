AWS re:Invent runs November 30 through December 4 in Las Vegas. Amazon says there are more than 2,200 sessions this year, plus five keynotes.

Nobody's watching all of that, including the people who are there. If you're following from home, the trick is the same one we used for [WWDC's session videos](/blog/wwdc-sessions-nobody-watches-real-announcements) in June: read first, and save your full hours for the handful of sessions that deserve them.

## What You'll Actually Be Able to Watch

The 2,200 number is everything on the schedule, and about 70% of it is interactive: workshops, builders' sessions, small-group formats built for the room. What ends up online is a smaller set. Last year, the keynotes, innovation talks and breakout sessions were up on demand by the Monday after the event, on AWS's site and the AWS Events YouTube channel.

That's still a lot of video, and most breakouts run about an hour. Here's the week's keynote lineup (all times Pacific):

- **Monday, Nov. 30, 7:30 p.m.:** Developer keynote with Marc Brooker
- **Tuesday, Dec. 1, 8:30 a.m.:** CEO keynote with Matt Garman
- **Wednesday, Dec. 2, 8:30 a.m.:** Data and AI keynote with Swami Sivasubramanian
- **Wednesday, Dec. 2, 3 p.m.:** Partner keynote with Ruba Borno
- **Thursday, Dec. 3, 8:30 a.m.:** Technology and innovations keynote with Matt Wood

## Don't Put the Whole Channel on a Schedule

Here's the one thing to avoid. AI Recaps schedules summarize every new upload from a channel, up to your plan's monthly summary limit. During re:Invent week, the AWS Events channel posts a lot of videos. Add it to a schedule and one run can use most of your month on sessions you'd never have picked.

Pick by hand instead.

## Step One: Shortlist by Title

When the recordings go up, skim the titles and session codes. Each session has a code: a few letters for the topic, then a number. The first digit of the number is usually the level, from 100 (intro) up to 400 (expert) and beyond. Skip anything outside your work, and anything pitched at a level that's too basic or too deep for you.

Aim for 15 to 20 that look relevant. Add the keynote or two closest to what you do.

## Step Two: Summarize the Shortlist

In AI Recaps, open **Summarize Now**. Search for the session, or tap "Can't find your video? Paste a URL instead" and paste the YouTube link. Hit **Generate**.

Each one counts toward your monthly limit, so 20 sessions is 20 summaries. That's the point of the shortlist.

Read the summaries and sort them:

- **Got it.** The summary covered what you needed: the launch, the feature, the pricing change.
- **Watch.** The summary shows there's a demo, an architecture walkthrough or a customer story with real detail. That's the stuff a summary can only point at.
- **Skip.** It wasn't what the title promised.

If you're on Pro, open any summary and ask the chat a direct question, like "does this cover cross-region setup?" It answers from the session's transcript, so you can settle a maybe without scrubbing through an hour.

## Step Three: Watch Five

Your Watch pile should be short. Five is a good number. Put them on over the next couple of weeks and give each one your full attention.

A summary tells you what was announced. It won't show you the diagram on slide 30 or the part of the demo that broke. We wrote about that tradeoff in [what a good summary catches and what it misses](/blog/what-a-good-summary-catches-and-misses).
