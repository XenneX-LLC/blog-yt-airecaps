The MLB trade deadline lands July 31, and the week leading into it is genuinely one of the most information-dense stretches on the sports calendar — without a single game being played that matters to the outcome. Here's the digest to bookmark and check back on as the week unfolds.

## Why This Week Matters More Than It Looks Like It Should

By this point in the season, every team has a clear read on its own position. Contenders know they're contenders. Sellers know they're sellers. The teams in between — close enough to the playoff picture to dream, far enough to have real trade chips worth moving — are where the actual drama concentrates, because those front offices are making a real, live decision about which direction to go.

The deadline itself has real teeth: after July 31, rosters are mostly locked for the stretch run. A team that needed bullpen help and didn't get it is stuck with what it has. This finality is what makes the week leading into it so dense — every team knows the window is closing, and it shows in how fast rumors move.

## What to Actually Watch For

**The bullpen market.** Relief pitching is almost always the most heavily traded category at the deadline, because contenders consistently underestimate how much bullpen depth they'll need for a playoff push, and sellers with a good reliever on an expiring contract have the cleanest trade chip available. If you want to predict where the action concentrates, start here.

**Rental players vs. multi-year control.** A "rental" — a player who's a free agent at the end of the season — costs less in trade value but only helps for the stretch run. A player with multiple years of team control costs significantly more but represents a longer-term bet. Watching which teams are willing to pay the multi-year price tells you who's genuinely building for sustained contention versus who's making a one-year push.

**The teams that make no moves.** Sometimes the most telling deadline story is a team that was expected to sell and didn't, or a contender that stayed quiet. Both usually signal something about the front office's read on their own roster that's worth paying attention to, even though it doesn't generate a highlight.

## How to Follow Without Doom-Scrolling Team Twitter

**Check once in the morning, once in the evening.** Deadline week generates near-constant rumor volume, most of which doesn't turn into an actual trade. Checking twice a day catches the real news without the noise of every unconfirmed report in between.

**Follow the reporters, not just the team accounts.** The insiders who consistently break real trades (rather than repeating speculation) are worth following directly — their track record tells you which rumors to actually take seriously.

**Wait for the deadline day roundup.** If you only have ten minutes for the whole week, skip the daily churn and read the July 31 end-of-day roundup once the deadline passes and the dust settles. That single piece will cover every deal that actually happened, which is the only part that matters in hindsight.

## The Bottom Line

Trade deadline week rewards checking in periodically rather than following continuously — the volume of speculation vastly outweighs the volume of actual trades until the final hours, when things move fast.
