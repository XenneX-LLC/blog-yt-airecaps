If you use YouTube a lot, you've probably noticed an Ask button showing up under some videos. YouTube has been building AI into the app for a while now, and in 2026 it got serious about it. A fair question for anyone who uses AI Recaps: if YouTube does this itself now, what's the point of a separate summary?

It's a good question, and the honest answer is that YouTube's AI is useful. It's just built for a different job.

## What YouTube's AI Actually Does

There are two features with almost the same name, and they're easy to mix up.

**Ask YouTube on the watch page.** This is the button under a video. You can ask a question about the video you're watching, or tap a suggested prompt like "recommend related content." It's available in a lot of countries and languages, but YouTube says it only appears on select videos, and its help page notes that "quality and accuracy may vary."

**Ask YouTube search.** Announced at Google I/O in May, this is conversational search across all of YouTube. You type a full question instead of keywords, and it pulls together Shorts and long videos to answer it, with follow-up questions to narrow things down. It launched as a test for U.S. Premium members 18 and older.

At its Made On YouTube event on September 23, YouTube said Ask YouTube is becoming a shopping companion too. Search for a product and you might get a comparison table built from creator reviews. YouTube also said more than 140 million people used Ask YouTube in June.

## Where It Does a Good Job

**Finding a specific moment.** YouTube says its model maps your question to the exact second of the video that answers it. If you're halfway through a repair video and want to know which screwdriver they used, that's the right tool.

**Finding videos you didn't know existed.** Ask YouTube search is built for messy questions that keywords handle badly. That's discovery, and it's a real improvement over a list of ten thumbnails.

We're not going to pretend those aren't good features. They are.

## What It Still Doesn't Do

**It waits for you to ask.** Both tools are question and answer. You have to know what to ask, and you have to be watching the video already. A summary works the other way around: it tells you what the video says before you've decided whether to watch it. As we wrote about [chapters](/blog/youtube-chapters-arent-a-summary), knowing where things are in a video isn't the same as knowing what it says.

**It's not on every video.** The watch-page tool shows up on the videos YouTube selects. With AI Recaps, you pick the video: paste a public YouTube link and it gets summarized.

**It doesn't come to you.** YouTube's AI lives inside YouTube, one video at a time. With AI Recaps you can follow channels and get summaries of everything they've posted since your last one, on a schedule you set, with an optional email digest. You don't have to open the app to find out whether a new video was worth your time.

**It doesn't give you the same shape every time.** A chat answer depends on the question. Every AI Recaps summary follows the same structure: a TL;DR, key points, a fuller summary, and notable quotes, with sponsor segments kept out of the main content. [Here's how we decide what goes in](/blog/how-ai-recaps-decides-what-makes-the-cut). The same shape is what makes it fast to skim ten summaries in a row.

**It doesn't keep a record.** Your summaries stay in your history, so you can come back to what a video said weeks later without rewatching it or asking again.

## Use Both

These tools don't compete as much as it seems. If you're already watching a video and want one answer, YouTube's Ask button is right there. If you're trying to decide what to watch, keep up with a dozen channels, or remember what a video said last month, that's what a summary is for.

YouTube's AI helps you get more out of a video you've already chosen. AI Recaps helps you choose.
