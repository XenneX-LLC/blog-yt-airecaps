The point of AI Recaps is that you don't have to keep checking. You pick your channels, pick a schedule, and the summaries show up. The schedule decides how often that happens, and it's worth a minute of thought, because the wrong one either buries you or leaves you behind.

## How Schedules Work

You get one schedule for your YouTube channels and one for your podcasts. Every channel on the YouTube schedule follows the same rhythm, and so does every show on the podcast schedule.

When you set one up, you pick how often it runs and when it starts. The start time sets the time of day your summaries arrive. For weekly and every-two-weeks schedules, the start date also sets the day of the week, and for monthly, the day of the month.

## The Options

**Daily.** A fresh batch every day at your chosen time. Good for a short list of channels that post often.

**Every Other Day.** Same idea, every two days. A good middle ground if daily feels like a lot.

**Weekly.** One round-up a week. This is the "Sunday morning coffee" schedule: everything from the past seven days, read in one sitting.

**Bi-Weekly.** Every two weeks. Useful for channels that post once or twice a month, where weekly would mostly come up empty.

**Monthly.** One complete overview a month. Best for slow channels, or for a list you want to keep an eye on without it becoming another inbox.

**As Released.** No fixed time. AI Recaps checks your YouTube channels every 15 minutes and summarizes new videos as they come out. So you don't get a notification every time, the alerts are bundled into one a day, sent around 7 p.m. your time. If the first new video of the day lands between 7 and 9 p.m., the alert goes out right away instead of waiting until tomorrow.

## Which Plan Gets What

Not every option is on every plan. On the free plan, you can choose Weekly, Bi-Weekly, Monthly or As Released. Daily and Every Other Day come with the paid plans. The free plan also covers up to three channels, so a weekly schedule over a short list is the natural fit there.

## Picking the Right One

The question to ask is how much your channels post, not how much you want to read.

- **A few channels that post daily**, like news or tech: Daily or Every Other Day keeps each batch short.
- **A mix of channels that post a few times a week:** Weekly. One round-up, read in one sitting, is the schedule most people stick with.
- **Channels that post rarely:** Bi-Weekly or Monthly. You'll still catch everything, and each summary batch will actually have something in it. If you've fallen behind on one, here's how we handle [catching up on a missed month](/blog/catching-up-on-a-missed-month-of-your-favorite-channel).
- **A list you want the minute it updates:** As Released, with the once-a-day alert.

If the schedule starts to feel like a chore, that's usually a sign the list has grown, not that the schedule is wrong. We wrote about [how people cope with too many subscriptions](/blog/how-people-cope-with-too-many-subscriptions), and trimming the list often does more than changing the timing.

## Podcasts Have One Extra Option

In the AI Recaps podcast app, podcasts get their own schedule with the same choices. On top of that, each show has its own setting: "Follow your schedule" or "At each new episode." Switch a show to "At each new episode" and it comes off your regular schedule and gets summarized whenever a new episode drops.

That's the closest thing to a per-show schedule. Put the one podcast you never miss on new episodes, and let the rest arrive on your regular schedule. Podcasts have their own kind of [backlog problem](/blog/the-podcast-backlog-is-worse-than-youtube), and this is the easiest way to keep one show from getting lost in it.

## Change It Whenever

None of this is permanent. If weekly turns out to be too slow, switch to every other day. If daily is piling up, go back to weekly. Your channels stay the same, and the next batch follows the new schedule.
