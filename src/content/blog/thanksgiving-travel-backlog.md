You leave Wednesday, you get back Sunday night, and your channels kept posting the whole time. Nobody takes a long weekend off YouTube except you.

Four days isn't a month. It's not a crisis. But it's long enough that the subscriptions page turns into a wall of thumbnails, and the usual reaction is to either start at the top and binge, or scroll past all of it and pretend it never happened. Neither one works. Here's a better way, and most of it happens before you leave.

## Before You Leave: Check Your Schedule

AI Recaps runs on a schedule, and it keeps running while you're away. Each run picks up the new videos your channels posted since the last one and summarizes them. Nothing gets skipped because you weren't looking.

So the question isn't whether your summaries will be there. It's how they'll arrive.

- **On Daily or Every Other Day,** you'll come home to several small batches.
- **On Weekly,** you might come home to one round-up that covers the whole trip, depending on where your schedule day falls.
- **On As Released,** summaries come in as videos post, with one alert a day.

Any of these works. If daily alerts during dinner with your family sound like a bad idea, switch to Weekly for the trip and switch back when you're home. We covered [how each schedule works](/blog/picking-your-summary-schedule) earlier this month, and changing it takes a few seconds.

One more check: if you're close to your plan's monthly summary limit, scheduled runs stop when you reach it. The end of November is a bad time to find that out.

## When You Get Home: Read Before You Watch

Open My Summaries and resist the urge to start with the newest video. Read through everything from the trip first. Four days from a normal subscription list is usually somewhere between a dozen and a few dozen videos, and the summaries for all of them take less time than one of the videos.

As you read, sort each one into one of three piles:

**Done.** The summary told you what you needed. News that's already old, a product update, a video you were only half interested in. You're caught up on it without watching it.

**Watch.** The summary made you want the real thing. A build you want to see, a conversation with real depth, a creator you watch for the delivery, not just the information.

**Skip.** Not for you this time. That's fine.

The Watch pile is your actual backlog, and it's almost always short. That's the part people miss when they come home to 40 new videos. You don't have 40 videos to watch. You have 40 to decide about, and maybe five to watch.

## Watch the Short List, Then Stop

Put the Watch pile on during the slow part of Monday, or save it to Watch Later and give it a real evening. Either way, don't let it grow. If something from the trip didn't make the Watch pile on the first pass, it's not going to get more urgent next week.

If you already had a Watch Later problem before Thanksgiving, this is a good week to deal with it too. The same read-first approach works on [a Watch Later list of any size](/blog/watch-later-5000-video-ceiling), and a trip is a natural reset point.

## This Is the Short Version of a Bigger Habit

A long weekend away is the small version of the problem. A month away is the big one, and we wrote about [catching up on a missed month of a favorite channel](/blog/catching-up-on-a-missed-month-of-your-favorite-channel) when that happens. The approach is the same at any size: read the summaries, decide, and only watch what earns it.

The holidays are about to stack up, too. Thanksgiving is the first of several long weekends between now and New Year's, and the same plan works for every one of them.
